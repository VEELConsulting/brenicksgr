Bauhaus for the back office

A school founded in 1919 already had the answer to the paper field ticket.

MON NOV 163 SIPSTHE BRENICKS ATELIER

This plate is sealed until Mon Nov 16. Read the open plates →

Sip one

The Bauhaus opened in Weimar in 1919 under the architect Walter Gropius. Its big idea was unglamorous: bring art and industry together and make everyday things well. Chairs, lamps, teapots, typefaces.

No ornament for its own sake. If a shape didn't help the thing work, it went.

Sip two

Most AI conversations start in the clouds. We start with the teapot: the one thing your team touches fifty times a day. The field ticket. The quote request. The Monday report.

Make that one object excellent (fast, clear, hard to fill in wrong) and the whole business feels different. It's Bauhaus thinking applied to operations: design the ordinary until it's beautiful because it works.

Design the ordinary until it's beautiful because it works.

Sip three

The school moved to Dessau in 1925 and closed in 1933 under pressure from the Nazi regime. Its teachers scattered across the world and took the ideas with them, which is why so much modern design still looks Bauhaus.

Good systems travel the same way. Build one well-made desk and it becomes the template for every branch, crew and client after it.
