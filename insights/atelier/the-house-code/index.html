Quiet dial, loud movement

Why the best work looks calm from the outside and never stops moving on the inside.

WED NOV 113 SIPSLIONEL K MUNYUKI, FOUNDER

This plate is sealed until Wed Nov 11. Read the open plates →

Sip one

A fine watch is calm. Two hands, a clean dial, nothing shouting for attention. Turn it over and there are dozens of parts working against time, finished by hand even where nobody will ever look.

That's the house code at Brenicks. The client sees a clean screen: one desk, one button, a Monday report in plain English. Behind it, agents read, check, file and flag all day long.

Sip two

We build things once, properly, and then we reuse them. The ticket desk built for one crew becomes the skeleton for the next one. Every week it gets a little better. That's kaizen: small improvements, forever.

It's also our moat. Work that's hard to build well is hard to copy. We'd rather do the difficult thing once than the easy thing every Tuesday.

The client sees a clean dial. The movement never stops.

Sip three

Dumas wrote about a man locked away for years who comes out patient, prepared and unstoppable. We like that arc for businesses too. Plenty of good companies are quietly held back by paper, fax and spreadsheets. Give them the right movement behind the dial and they move fast.

That's what this journal is for: the artists and schools that shape how we design, and what each one would teach a business owner over three sips of coffee.

Written by Lionel K Munyuki, Founder
